# The Infinite Body — beta access landing page prompt

Create the copy and section-by-section layout for a single landing page for **The Infinite Body training app beta**. The page should attract people who will use the app during real strength sessions and give feedback. The primary action is **Request beta access**. Treat `/beta/` as a proposed URL.

## Audience and message

Write for busy professionals who already strength train and for recreational lifters with some training experience. They want to know what to do when they arrive at the gym, what weight to use, and whether they are making progress across training blocks. They have limited attention between sets and training weeks that can be disrupted by work or travel.

Lead with a concrete session-to-session benefit. Use this draft as a starting point.

> **Know what to train next.**
>
> Try The Infinite Body training app beta. Open your next session, follow your plan set by set, and track how each workout fits into your training year.
>
> **Request beta access**  
> Access is by invitation. We’ll email you if a place is available.

Keep the tone direct, practical, and grounded in strength training. Use short sentences and translate programming terms into things a lifter can see or do. Introduce the founder and the coaching method only where they help explain how the app works.

## Features to lead with

| Priority | Feature to show | Why a tester would care |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **This Week and the guided workout**. See the scheduled session, tap **Start Workout**, and follow it on your phone. The runner shows prescribed sets, reps, tempo and rest, with a rest timer and exercise demos where available. Warm-ups, supersets and conditioning appear when prescribed. | Know the next exercise and how to perform it without juggling a PDF, notes and a timer. |
| 2 | **Suggested weights from your training numbers**. Eligible prescriptions can show load suggestions based on strength inputs or logged work. Record the weight and reps you actually perform. | Have a sensible starting point for the next set and a record of what you did. |
| 3 | **My Program and the annual training calendar**. See the current phase, its goal, your week in the block and the sessions assigned so far. Use the year view and green completion ticks to see the work you’ve logged across training blocks. | Know where you are in the plan and see consistency over time. |
| 4 | **Strength history and progress**. View recorded sets, estimated rep maxes and strength trends where enough data is available. Coach-led movement assessments can give clients another reference point where assigned. | See changes in performance across sessions and phases. |

Make the first three the main feature cards. Use the fourth as a short supporting section. Describe load suggestions as estimates and show what they depend on. Keep assessments secondary because a coach assigns and records them.

## Page structure

1. **Hero.** Brief value proposition, one annotated app screenshot, and the **Request beta access** button linking to the form.
2. **See the app in use.** Show three moments in order. Find this week’s session, run and log a workout, then check it off in My Program. Use screenshots and captions that show those exact actions.
3. **What beta testers do.** Ask people to use the app in a few normal strength sessions and tell us where the instructions, suggested weights or logging were unclear. Explain that TIB reviews requests and sends invitations by email. Approved testers receive complimentary app access while they’re in the beta. Confirm separately whether a test program or any coaching is included before offering either.
4. **Request access.** Require a name and email address. Offer a short optional field such as “What would you most like to test?” Add a device question only if it helps assign testers to supported builds. Use visible field labels, clear validation, and a privacy-policy link. Explain that the email is used to respond about the beta. Show a success message only after the request is received, with a clear retry message if submission fails.
5. **FAQ and final CTA.** Answer who the beta is for, what participation involves, whether it costs anything during the beta, and when people will hear back. List only the devices and builds confirmed for the beta. Use **Request beta access** again.

## Visual direction and constraints

- Match the existing marketing site in `index-v2.html`. Use Poppins headings, Hanken Grotesk body text, charcoal and off-white surfaces, and the site’s orange accent (`#ff6500`). Keep text and buttons legible at mobile widths and use green completion ticks with a text label.
- Use approved, anonymised screenshots from the actual app, ideally the phone workout runner and the My Program year view. The marketing repo’s `assets/app-week-matrix.jpg` and `assets/app-session-detail.jpg` are starting references. Use a labelled placeholder wherever a current screenshot is needed.
- Keep the page focused on the invite request. Treat testimonials, performance claims, spots, dates and platform availability as approved facts to confirm before publishing. Keep the form free of health details, payment fields and automatic account creation.
- Coordinate the existing homepage wording about coaching-client access with the invite-only beta when the page launches.

## Return

Provide ready-to-use landing page copy, a section outline, suggested screenshot captions, form labels and success and error messages, and a page title and meta description. End by listing the launch decisions still open, including supported devices, what an invitation includes, who reviews requests, and where submissions go.
