How to Plan Your Strength Training Week.
Written by David Robertson, Online Strength Coach.

Already following a strength program, but struggling to fit its sessions into your week? Start by comparing the training you planned with the training you completed.

The biggest planning mistake I see in strength training is committing to a training program without deciding when its sessions will happen.

A training matrix is a simple table showing which workout belongs on which day. In the article's fictional example, Alex has four strength sessions each week. He plans an upper-body session on Monday, a lower-body session on Tuesday, another upper-body session on Thursday, and another lower-body session on Friday. Wednesday and the weekend are days off. He uses this schedule for three weeks.

This is an illustration. Session order, frequency, and spacing depend on the trainee and the program.

At the end of three weeks, Alex reviews what happened. In week one, he completed all four planned strength sessions. In week two, he completed three, missing Tuesday because of a meeting. In week three, he completed three again, missing Tuesday because of a work deadline.

That adds up to ten of twelve planned strength sessions, or about eighty-three percent. The useful finding is that both missed sessions were Tuesday's lower-body workout. His overall attendance hides a repeated gap in the work tied to his squat goal.

When the same session repeatedly gets missed, review whether its timing and demands fit your week. Three weeks gives you a practical starting point for that review.

Your weekly matrix sets out when the sessions happen. Periodization means planning how the training itself changes over time, including the amount of work and how heavy it is.
