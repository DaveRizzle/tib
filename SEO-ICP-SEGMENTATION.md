# SEO Segmentation and Ideal Customer Profile Research

**Site:** The Infinite Body  
**Prepared:** 13 November 2026  
**Status:** Working research plan — hypotheses, not approved positioning  
**Companion document:** `SEO-SCOPE-OF-WORK.md`

## Purpose

Refine The Infinite Body's niche by testing which customer situation has the strongest
combination of pain, reachability, willingness to pay, service fit and retention. This
work must be completed before building a large permanent SEO page architecture.

The objective is not to manufacture a distinctive persona. It is to find the overlap
between:

1. David's authentic experience and coaching strengths;
2. a recurring and expensive customer problem;
3. an outcome customers value enough to purchase;
4. an audience The Infinite Body can reliably reach;
5. a service The Infinite Body can deliver repeatedly and credibly.

SEO is one source of evidence. Search volume alone does not define the niche.

## Business boundary

This document covers **online strength coaching only**.

David's data-analysis background can support a differentiated coaching method, but the
site should express it as a client benefit: better-informed decisions and less
programming complexity for the client. Career development in data science and separate
AI learning-and-development services are different search markets and should not be
mixed into The Infinite Body's SEO architecture.

If AI training becomes a commercial offer, it requires a separate positioning,
research and web-property decision.

## Working positioning territory

> Data-informed, adaptive strength coaching for people whose demanding professional
> lives make fixed training programmes fail.

This is a research territory, not final homepage copy. The customer-facing promise
should emphasise simplicity and outcomes rather than requiring clients to understand
internal load, external load or statistical modelling.

A useful translation is:

> Your workload, travel and recovery change. Your training should adapt without
> requiring you to become your own programme manager.

## Hypothesis format

Use this structure consistently:

> When **[specific situation or trigger]**, **[specific audience]** will pay for
> **[offer or mechanism]** to achieve **[valuable outcome]**, because
> **[current alternative]** fails due to **[constraint]**.

Avoid hypotheses based only on age, company size, job title or an assumed keyword.
Identity can help with reach and message recognition, but the situation and job-to-be-
done explain why someone buys.

## Hypothesis 1 — Busy senior professionals

### Testable hypothesis

> When demanding work, travel or changing schedules repeatedly disrupt training,
> senior professionals who value strength will pay for ongoing adaptive online
> coaching to sustain progress without personally redesigning every training week,
> because generic programmes assume stable schedules and leave the trainee to solve
> every disruption.

### Provisional ICP

| Attribute | Working definition |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Executive, senior leader, founder, partner or established professional |
| Training maturity | Has trained seriously before; understands basic lifts and does not need to be persuaded that strength matters |
| Situation | Workload, travel and schedule variability repeatedly interrupt otherwise valued training |
| Trigger | Promotion, increased travel, return after a demanding period, repeated programme failure, approaching a personally important milestone |
| Core job | Keep strength training progressing while outsourcing programme adaptation and decision-making |
| Functional outcome | A realistic programme that changes with available time, equipment, fatigue and training history |
| Emotional outcome | Confidence that a disrupted week has not ruined the entire plan |
| Social/identity outcome | Remain capable and disciplined without fitness becoming another executive project to manage |
| Failed alternatives | Static templates, self-programming, app-only plans, repeatedly restarting generic programmes |
| Likely objection | “I cannot guarantee a stable schedule, so coaching may be wasted on me.” |
| Value mechanism | Coach absorbs complexity and adapts the plan instead of demanding perfect compliance |
| Potential willingness-to-pay signal | Already spends on convenience, expertise, health, coaching or high-quality professional services |

### Inclusion indicators

- Strength training is already personally important.
- The prospect has experienced repeated disruption rather than a temporary busy week.
- They want coaching judgement, not merely a workout PDF.
- They can train with some regularity even if schedule and equipment vary.
- They value reduced decision load and continuity.

### Exclusion indicators

- Primarily wants rapid weight loss or a short generic challenge.
- Has no realistic opportunity or willingness to train.
- Requires diagnosis, rehabilitation or medical treatment outside coaching scope.
- Wants guaranteed injury prevention or a guaranteed performance result.
- Wants an entirely self-service, lowest-price programme.

### Language to investigate

Do not assume prospects search using “executive.” Test identity and situation language:

- executive fitness coach;
- strength coach for busy professionals;
- online personal trainer for executives;
- strength training with a busy schedule;
- training around business travel;
- maintain strength while travelling;
- workout programme for unpredictable schedule;
- how to stay consistent with training when work is busy.

### Reach hypotheses

- Existing professional network and former colleagues;
- executive and founder communities;
- LinkedIn content and direct referrals;
- professional-service partnerships;
- search demand around travel, schedule and consistency problems.

### Evidence required

- Repeated switching stories involving schedule variability.
- Evidence that programme adaptation—not merely motivation—is valued.
- Examples of money or substantial time already spent on failed alternatives.
- Qualified bookings from message tests.
- Retention through at least one demanding work/travel period.

## Hypothesis 2 — Intermediate lifters repeatedly stalled by changing constraints

### Testable hypothesis

> When progress repeatedly stalls because life, fatigue and training availability do
> not match a fixed programme, intermediate strength trainees will pay for adaptive
> coaching to progress consistently without repeatedly restarting, because templates
> cannot incorporate their individual history and changing constraints.

### Provisional ICP

| Attribute | Working definition |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Intermediate recreational strength trainee or strength-oriented athlete |
| Training maturity | Has meaningful training history and can perform the main movements independently |
| Situation | Progress has stalled; training cycles are abandoned or repeatedly reset |
| Trigger | Failed programme cycle, recurring interruption, persistent plateau, return after a layoff, frustration with self-programming |
| Core job | Turn inconsistent effort into a sustainable progression process |
| Functional outcome | Appropriate progression, exercise selection and load changes based on actual response |
| Emotional outcome | Stop interpreting every disruption or poor session as failure |
| Failed alternatives | Popular templates, programme hopping, copying advanced athletes, self-adjustment without a clear model |
| Likely objection | “I already know how to train; I should be able to solve this myself.” |
| Value mechanism | Longitudinal coaching judgement, accountability and adaptation based on training response |
| Potential willingness-to-pay signal | Has bought programmes, equipment, memberships, courses or previous coaching |

### Inclusion indicators

- Can describe a specific plateau or repeated failure pattern.
- Has enough training history for individual adaptation to matter.
- Is willing to log training and communicate constraints honestly.
- Values long-term progress more than novelty.
- Wants coaching decisions and feedback, not only accountability.

### Exclusion indicators

- Complete beginner for whom a simple standard programme is sufficient.
- Constantly seeks programme novelty rather than following a process.
- Primarily requires clinical rehabilitation.
- Unwilling to record enough information for remote coaching.
- Expects advanced outcomes without consistent training participation.

### Language to investigate

- strength training plateau;
- intermediate strength programme;
- why do I keep stalling on my lifts;
- strength programme that adapts;
- online strength programming;
- personalised strength programme;
- keep restarting workout programme;
- how to adjust training when life gets busy.

### Reach hypotheses

- Strength-training communities and specialist forums;
- YouTube audiences already represented in the market-intelligence research;
- referrals from gyms, physiotherapists and coaches whose scope differs;
- search demand around plateaus, programme adjustment and intermediate programming.

### Evidence required

- Specific examples of failed templates and programme switching.
- Proof that the plateau is painful enough to pay to solve.
- A repeatable coaching mechanism that can be demonstrated through case studies.
- Better activation and retention than broad “online coaching” clients.
- Search terms with both problem relevance and a plausible coaching conversion path.

## Hypothesis 3 — Data-oriented trainees seeking transparent coaching

### Testable hypothesis

> When generic coaching feels opaque or arbitrary, experienced and analytically minded
> trainees will pay for evidence-informed online coaching that explains decisions and
> adapts to their data, because generic plans and black-box apps do not provide enough
> context or trust.

### Provisional ICP

| Attribute | Working definition |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Analytically minded professional, coach or experienced trainee |
| Training maturity | Comfortable tracking training and discussing programme decisions |
| Situation | Dissatisfied with generic plans, unexplained changes or superficial app metrics |
| Trigger | Poor experience with generic coaching/app, unexplained plateau, desire to understand a training response |
| Core job | Receive individual coaching decisions that are both practical and intelligible |
| Functional outcome | Training adjustments grounded in history, performance and context |
| Emotional outcome | Trust that programme changes are reasoned rather than arbitrary |
| Failed alternatives | Black-box apps, generic dashboards, overcomplicated self-analysis, coaches who cannot explain decisions |
| Likely objection | “Will this add more tracking and analysis than it saves?” |
| Value mechanism | Coach performs the analysis and communicates only the detail useful to the client |
| Potential willingness-to-pay signal | Already tracks training, pays for specialist tools or seeks evidence-led practitioners |

### Inclusion indicators

- Values explanation and transparency but still wants the coach to make decisions.
- Has usable training history or is willing to establish a simple baseline.
- Understands that data informs judgement rather than guaranteeing outcomes.
- Values evidence without expecting certainty from every metric.

### Exclusion indicators

- Wants to debate every adjustment rather than be coached.
- Seeks a dashboard or analytics product rather than strength coaching.
- Expects prediction certainty unsupported by training data.
- Finds any tracking unacceptable.
- Requires medical monitoring outside coaching scope.

### Language to investigate

- evidence-based strength coach;
- data-driven strength training;
- evidence-based online coaching;
- personalised strength programming;
- training-load monitoring for strength training;
- adaptive strength coaching;
- online coach who uses training data.

### Reach hypotheses

- Technical and analytical professional communities;
- evidence-based training audiences;
- long-form technical articles, demonstrations and case studies;
- coaches and experienced trainees seeking a second perspective.

### Evidence required

- Buyers explicitly cite transparency or evidence as a selection criterion.
- Prospects value the method without demanding excessive custom reporting.
- Message tests produce qualified coaching interest rather than software/product
  interest.
- Retention demonstrates that explanation improves trust and adherence.

### Key risk

This may be a **message preference within Hypotheses 1 and 2**, not a standalone niche.
Research must test whether it predicts purchase and retention or merely describes an
interesting content audience.

## Relationship between the hypotheses

The hypotheses are not assumed to be mutually exclusive.

A likely eventual ICP may be:

> An intermediate strength trainee in a demanding senior-professional role whose
> variable schedule repeatedly breaks fixed programmes and who values transparent,
> evidence-informed coaching.

That intersection is attractive but may be too narrow or expressed in language no one
searches for. Research should establish whether combining the traits increases buying
pull or merely produces an elegant description.

Maintain separate fields for:

- professional identity;
- triggering situation;
- training maturity;
- primary job-to-be-done;
- preferred level of technical explanation.

Do not merge them into one segment prematurely.

## Research sequence

### Stage 1 — Existing evidence inventory

Before recruiting interviews, collect what already exists:

- previous and current client profiles;
- enquiry and sales-call notes;
- reasons clients started, stayed, paused or left;
- training constraints repeatedly mentioned;
- prior programme/coaching alternatives;
- testimonials and outcome evidence;
- referral sources;
- retention and purchase history where available.

Record observations without forcing them into the current hypotheses.

### Stage 2 — Initial interviews

Conduct approximately 12–15 interviews distributed across the three hypotheses. Do not
commit to 10–15 per segment until recurring patterns justify deeper research.

Recruit from:

- current or recent paying clients;
- former clients;
- serious near-buyers who did not proceed;
- people who recently purchased an alternative;
- referrals matching the situation but unfamiliar with the brand.

Avoid relying only on friends, fans or highly satisfied existing clients.

### Stage 3 — Customer-language mining

Collect exact phrases from:

- interview transcripts;
- enquiry emails and messages;
- sales calls;
- relevant Reddit and forum discussions;
- comments under relevant long-form content;
- competitor testimonials;
- competitor 1–3 star reviews;
- reviews of static programmes and coaching apps.

Tag each phrase by trigger, problem, failed alternative, desired outcome, objection and
purchase criterion. Preserve the original wording rather than translating everything
into coaching terminology.

### Stage 4 — Search-demand validation

For each repeated customer phrase, examine:

- query volume where available;
- search-result intent;
- ranking page types;
- commercial versus informational character;
- relevance to the actual offer;
- competition and authority requirements;
- whether the phrase is identity-, problem-, trigger- or outcome-led.

A low-volume query may still be valuable if it closely predicts a qualified buyer. A
high-volume query is not valuable if it primarily attracts beginners, students,
clinicians or people seeking free templates.

### Stage 5 — Message tests

Create controlled, non-indexed landing-page variants using the same underlying offer
but different segment framing. Do not create many thin indexed pages.

Hold constant where practical:

- offer and price;
- primary CTA;
- booking process;
- page quality and proof;
- traffic source and campaign controls.

Vary:

- audience/situation headline;
- trigger and problem framing;
- failed alternative;
- outcome emphasis;
- method explanation;
- proof most relevant to the segment.

Use small, deliberate paid campaigns only after conversion tracking is verified. Avoid
broad match, unreviewed automatic recommendations and page-view conversion goals.

### Stage 6 — Behaviour and retention

Evaluate more than landing-page conversion:

1. Qualified booking rate;
2. attendance rate;
3. sales conversion;
4. onboarding completion;
5. time to first experienced value;
6. retention through a relevant disruption or training cycle;
7. referral and testimonial willingness;
8. operational effort and service profitability.

Run the Sean Ellis question only with clients who have experienced the central service
value and when the sample is large enough to interpret. The 40% “very disappointed”
threshold is a heuristic, not proof by itself.

## Interview guide

Focus on the most recent real event. Do not pitch the hypothesis during the interview.

### Context

1. Tell me about your training before you started looking for help.
2. What else was happening in your work or life at the time?
3. How important was strength training relative to your other priorities?

### Switching moment

4. Tell me about the last occasion when your existing approach stopped working.
5. What happened that made the problem noticeable then?
6. Why did you act at that point rather than earlier?
7. Was there a specific session, result, interruption or conversation that triggered
   the search?

### Alternatives and workarounds

8. What did you try first?
9. What programmes, apps, coaches or personal systems had you already used?
10. What worked temporarily?
11. Where did each alternative fail?
12. What did the workaround cost in money, time, confidence or lost progress?

### Purchase criteria

13. What did you need to believe before paying for help?
14. What options did you compare?
15. What nearly stopped you from proceeding?
16. Who else influenced the decision?
17. What made one option feel safer or more credible?

### Desired outcome

18. What would meaningful progress have looked like six months later?
19. Which outcome mattered most: performance, consistency, reduced decision load,
    confidence, understanding, or something else?
20. How would you know the service was working before a major strength result appeared?

### Language and search behaviour

21. How did you describe the problem to another person?
22. What did you type into Google, YouTube or a community search?
23. Which words used by coaches felt relevant? Which sounded generic or exaggerated?

### Follow-up probes

Use neutral prompts:

- “What happened next?”
- “Can you give me a specific example?”
- “What made that difficult?”
- “How did you decide?”
- “What did you mean by that word?”

Avoid hypothetical questions such as “Would you buy…?” and leading questions such as
“Was your busy schedule the main problem?”

## Viability scorecard

Score only after documenting evidence. Use 1–5 for each dimension and include a source
or observation beside every score.

| Dimension | Weight | What counts as evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Pain intensity | 20% | Repeated disruption, meaningful consequences, active workarounds |
| Willingness to pay | 20% | Prior spend, deposits, paid pilots, accepted pricing |
| Reachability | 15% | Identifiable communities, referrals, lists or relevant queries |
| Service fit | 15% | Problem can be solved within coaching scope and current capabilities |
| Retention potential | 10% | Ongoing job, repeated value and observed client persistence |
| Competitive gap | 10% | Incumbents underserve the situation; evidence beyond generic claims |
| Search opportunity | 5% | Relevant intent and achievable result pages |
| Proof availability | 5% | Credible experience, cases or demonstrations relevant to the segment |

A high weighted score does not override a serious scope, ethics or delivery problem.

## Decision rules

A hypothesis advances when it has:

- repeated independent descriptions of the same trigger and job-to-be-done;
- demonstrated expenditure or costly workarounds;
- qualified behavioural response to its message;
- an offer The Infinite Body can deliver credibly;
- a reachable acquisition path;
- no dependence on unsupported injury or performance guarantees.

A hypothesis is revised or rejected when:

- interest is mostly polite or hypothetical;
- content engagement attracts people who do not want coaching;
- the problem is occasional and low-cost;
- the audience expects medical care or a software product;
- conversions disappear after removing test/internal traffic;
- acquisition requires broad, unrelated traffic;
- delivery complexity makes the segment unprofitable.

Do not declare a winning niche from a few bookings, a ranking increase or a small paid
campaign. Document uncertainty and define the next discriminating test.

## Messaging principles

1. Lead with the customer's situation, not David's self-discovery process.
2. Present technical capability as reduced complexity and better decisions for the
   client.
3. Use customer language where it remains accurate.
4. Do not promise injury prevention, medical outcomes or guaranteed strength gains.
5. Distinguish coaching judgement from an app, dashboard or static programme.
6. Show the mechanism and proof rather than repeatedly claiming “personalised” or
   “data-driven.”
7. Reserve deeper technical detail for methodology pages and expert articles.
8. Use the Newton quotation, if retained, in a founder essay or About context—not as
   the opening argument on a commercial landing page.

## SEO implications after validation

Once a segment demonstrates stronger pull:

- make the winning situation the primary commercial-page framing;
- build supporting content around validated triggers, problems and objections;
- preserve one primary page per distinct search intent;
- avoid indexed near-duplicate segment pages;
- use case studies that match the selected ICP;
- link informational content to the appropriate commercial decision;
- continue measuring confirmed appointments and retained clients, not traffic alone.

Possible permanent page types—subject to evidence—include:

- online strength coaching for busy professionals;
- how adaptive coaching works when schedules change;
- training around work travel and limited equipment;
- progressing beyond an intermediate strength plateau;
- evidence-informed training decisions without client-side complexity;
- relevant client case studies and coaching-process explanations.

These are research hypotheses, not approved keyword targets or publication briefs.
